Harry Kane Wins 2026 Ballon d’Or in Historic Night at London Palladium

By Trending9ja Staff • October 30, 2026 • 2 min read

Harry Kane made history on Monday night as he was crowned the 2026 Ballon d’Or winner at a glittering ceremony held at the London Palladium. The 33-year-old Bayern Munich and England captain became the first Englishman to lift the prestigious award since Michael Owen in 2001, ending a 25-year wait for English football.

Kane edged out Real Madrid’s Kylian Mbappé and Barcelona’s Lamine Yamal in a tight race that went down to the wire. The ceremony, the 70th edition of the award organised by France Football, was the first ever held outside France or Switzerland and paid tribute to the inaugural winner, Sir Stanley Matthews, in 1956. Hosts Kate Scott and Thierry Henry guided the evening, which was briefly delayed by heavy traffic and rain in London.

Kane’s Outstanding Season

Kane’s triumph was built on an extraordinary 2025-26 campaign. He scored 73 goals in 65 competitive matches for club and country. At Bayern Munich, he fired the Bavarians to a Bundesliga and DFB-Pokal double and helped them reach the Champions League semi-finals. For England, he led the Three Lions to the World Cup semi-finals, where they were knocked out by eventual champions Argentina.

Presented with the trophy by Brazilian legend Ronaldo, Kane looked emotional as the home crowd chanted his name. “Wow, wow, wow. I am almost speechless,” he began. “Being here in London has made it extra special. It looked like a good omen for me tonight.”

He thanked voters, his Bayern teammates (including Michael Olise, Dayot Upamecano, and Luis Díaz), England manager Thomas Tuchel, and the staff at both clubs. He paid special tribute to his wife Katie and their children, saying the award was the result of years of hard work, loans early in his career, and resilience.

After the ceremony, Kane shared the quote he has lived by: “Work hard in silence. Let success be your noise.”

Women’s Ballon d’Or and Other Awards

Spanish midfielder Alexia Putellas claimed the Women’s Ballon d’Or for a third time after another dominant season with Barcelona Femení before her move to London City Lionesses.

Other major winners included: Men’s Club of the Year: Paris Saint-Germain Women’s Club of the Year: Barcelona Femení Men’s Young Player (Kopa Trophy): Lamine Yamal (third consecutive win) Women’s Young Player: Vicky López Men’s Coach of the Year: Luis de la Fuente (Spain) Women’s Coach of the Year: Pere Romeu (Barcelona) Men’s Goalkeeper (Yashin Trophy): Unai Simón Women’s Goalkeeper: Cata Coll Sócrates Award: Bukayo Saka (Arsenal / England) Men’s Striker of the Year (Gerd Müller Trophy): Harry Kane Women’s Striker of the Year: Charlyn Corral (Mexico)
